## Research standard

For a serious new public product, search official websites, current app/extension stores, pricing pages, product release notes, and accessible user feedback. Search for **direct competitors and substitutes** (spreadsheets, websites, existing workflows), not only apps using the same keywords. Include links, region/currency, plan constraints, and date checked. Use a representative mix of positive, recent negative, and high-volume recurring complaints; acknowledge selection bias and uncertain review authenticity.

Do not treat ratings alone as proof of customer satisfaction. Do not invent market size, revenue, competitor pricing, review counts, or user demand. If no direct competitor is found, say **“none found in this search”**, not “no app exists.” A competitor doing the job well may be a strong reason to build a narrower personal tool or not enter that market commercially.

Research depth must match the decision and available credits. A weekend personal script does **not** need weeks of market research; an intended subscription app deserves more serious comparison. Do not commission expensive research, scrape restricted sources, or run paid external APIs without authorization. Recheck unstable competitor/pricing facts before major investment decisions.
